!!! definition "Definition 2.1.20 : Outer Product"
    Define $|w\rangle\langle v|$ to be the linear operator from $V$ to $W$ whose action is defined by

    $$
    \begin{equation*}
    (|w\rangle\langle v|)\left(\left|v^{\prime}\right\rangle\right) \equiv|w\rangle\left\langle v \mid v^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle v \mid v^{\prime}\right\rangle|w\rangle \tag{2.20}
    \end{equation*}
    $$

!!! definition "Definition 2.1.12 : Inner Product"
    An inner product on a complex vector space $V$ is a function $(\cdot, \cdot) : V \times V \to \mathbf{C}$ such that:

    1. It is linear in the second argument:
       $$
       \left(|v\rangle, \sum_i \lambda_i |w_i\rangle\right) = \sum_i \lambda_i (|v\rangle, |w_i\rangle).
       $$
    2. It satisfies conjugate symmetry:
       $$
       (|v\rangle, |w\rangle) = (|w\rangle, |v\rangle)^*.
       $$
    3. It is positive definite:
       $$
       (|v\rangle, |v\rangle) \ge 0,
       $$
       with equality if and only if $|v\rangle = 0$.

    In quantum mechanical notation, $(|v\rangle, |w\rangle) = \langle v \mid w\rangle$. A vector space equipped with an inner product is called an inner product space.

!!! theorem "Theorem 2.1.70 : Tensor product properties and operator tensor products"
    The tensor product satisfies:

    $$
    z(|v\rangle \otimes |w\rangle) = (z|v\rangle) \otimes |w\rangle = |v\rangle \otimes (z|w\rangle),
    $$

    $$
    (|v_1\rangle + |v_2\rangle) \otimes |w\rangle = |v_1\rangle \otimes |w\rangle + |v_2\rangle \otimes |w\rangle,
    $$

    $$
    |v\rangle \otimes (|w_1\rangle + |w_2\rangle) = |v\rangle \otimes |w_1\rangle + |v\rangle \otimes |w_2\rangle.
    $$

    If $A$ and $B$ are linear operators on $V$ and $W$, then $A \otimes B$ is the linear operator on $V \otimes W$ defined by

    $$
    (A \otimes B)(|v\rangle \otimes |w\rangle) \equiv A|v\rangle \otimes B|w\rangle,
    $$

    extended linearly to arbitrary elements of $V \otimes W$.

    More generally, any linear operator $C : V \otimes W \to V' \otimes W'$ can be written as

    $$
    C = \sum_i c_i A_i \otimes B_i.
    $$
